Dataset Interpretations & Strategic Agricultural Insights

Detailed analysis of query distributions to infer priority farmer concerns and emerging risk areas.

Priority Concerns (Intent Analysis)

Market Rates & Info (357 queries) and Plant Protection (348 queries) dominate farmer inquiries, accounting for 35.2% of all interactions. This suggests that the immediate survival of the farming enterprise depends on two factors: securing fair prices for their harvest (minimizing middleman exploitation) and combating diseases/pests before they result in total crop loss.

Seasonal Trends & Planning

Query volumes peak in January (768 queries) and February (695 queries) during the Rabi crop harvesting and early Kharif planning stages. Farmers inquire heavily about moisture conservation, late winter weather anomalies (rain and frost), and seed availability for subsequent sowing cycles, showing a clear alignment with regional agricultural calendars.

Emerging Risk Areas

A significant volume of queries in remote regions relates to pest infestation and sudden weather alerts. The lag in advisory services (averaging 18 days in Remote Blocks compared to 5 days in Urban-Near blocks) combined with low expert access creates a critical risk zone where unvalidated AI advisories can lead to crop failures.

Spoilage Dataset Interpretations & Diagnostics

Analyzing key factors contributing to crop post-harvest decay.

Crop Susceptibility

Onion is highly vulnerable to spoilage, showing a severe 69.6% decay rate. High moisture crops like Onion and Tomato (53.3%) require active ventilation to dry properly. Grains like Wheat (44.4%) are more stable due to their low natural humidity.

Temperature Influence

Spoilage rates peak at 60.7% in the 30°C – 35°C range. Warm, humid air accelerates respiration and fungal multiplication. In dry extreme heat (>35°C), the spoilage rate drops to 43.5% as rapid evaporation dehydrates cells before rot can spread.

Closed Storage Dangers

Crops stored inside a Storage Area spoiled more frequently (57.1%) than those kept outside (51.0%). This highlights that closed warehouses without active ventilation gather heat and relative humidity, creating a mold-breeding microclimate.

Ambiguity Solver & Model Refinement

Examine how overlapping words create language ambiguity and how our TF-IDF model resolves them.

Natural language queries often contain vocabulary terms that point to multiple intents. To resolve this ambiguity, we compute term importance using TF-IDF: terms unique to specific classes (like subsidy or mosaic) receive high IDF weights, while common conversational noise is filtered out.

Interactive Ambiguity Examples (Click to Test):

"What is the government subsidy available for purchasing wheat seeds?"
Conflicts: Schemes (subsidy) vs Seeds (wheat seeds).
Resolution: The term "subsidy" has an IDF of 3.2 (very rare/informative), which overpowers the term "seeds" (IDF of 2.1). Correctly classified as Government Schemes.
"Will rain affect the market rate of onion?"
Conflicts: Weather (rain) vs Market (market rate).
Resolution: "rain" (IDF 3.1) and "market" (IDF 2.2) both trigger. In centroid space, the query aligns slightly closer to Weather because winter weather queries contain high density of crop impacts.
"What fertilizer doses prevent blight attack on paddy?"
Conflicts: Nutrients (fertilizer doses) vs Protection (blight attack).
Resolution: "blight" and "attack" have extremely strong associations in the **Plant Protection** centroid, successfully routing it to protection rather than standard nutrients.
